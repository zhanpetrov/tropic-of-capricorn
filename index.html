The Epigraph to "Her" (June Mansfield)

Tropic of Capricorn opens with the dedication "To Her." Unlike Tropic of Cancer (set in 1930s Paris), Capricorn reaches back into Miller's 1920s Brooklyn and Manhattan years, charting the spiritual paralysis before he met his second wife, June Edith Smith ("Mara" / "Mona"), at Wilson's Dance Hall on Broadway.

OBELISK PRESS CONTRABAND DOSSIER

Banned for 23 Years: From Paris Smugglers to Grove Press (1939–1962)

First published in Paris by Jack Kahane's Obelisk Press in February 1939 at 16 Place Vendôme, Tropic of Capricorn bore a stark legal warning on its cover: "NOT TO BE IMPORTED INTO GREAT BRITAIN OR U.S.A."

For over two decades, American and British travelers smuggled green-and-buff paperbound copies past U.S. Customs inside coat linings and false-bottomed suitcases. Only in September 1962—after Barney Rosset's Grove Press waged a historic legal battle across dozens of U.S. courts—was Tropic of Capricorn legally published in Henry Miller's homeland.
